What is tranexamic acid in skin care?

Tranexamic acid is a synthetic derivative of the amino acid lysine. Medicine has used it for decades to reduce bleeding, because it blocks the lysine-binding sites on plasminogen and so slows the formation of plasmin. Dermatologists noticed that the same action also reduces pigmentation, and TXA is now studied orally, by injection and topically for melasma and other hyperpigmentation. A 2017 literature review in the American Journal of Clinical Dermatology concluded that TXA looks promising by every route, with results comparable to or better than standard therapies and fewer side effects, while noting that more controlled studies are needed.

In cosmetics, TXA is used for a simpler goal: a more even skin tone, fewer visible dark spots and less of the post-sun and post-acne darkening that Indian consumers complain about most. That is why "tranexamic acid serum", "tranexamic acid cream" and "tranexamic acid serum for pigmentation" all show steady search demand on Amazon India.

How does a tranexamic acid serum work?

Pigmentation is not only a melanocyte problem. When skin is exposed to UV light, keratinocytes make more plasminogen activator. Plasminogen is converted to plasmin, and plasmin releases signals such as arachidonic acid (which pushes melanocytes to make more melanin) and fibroblast growth factor (which encourages new blood vessels). This is why melasma patches are often slightly red and vascular as well as brown.

Tranexamic acid interrupts this chain early, at plasmin. Less plasmin means fewer pro-pigment signals reaching the melanocyte, and less vessel growth. The TranEX-1 TDS adds two further actions for its tranexamoyl peptide:

  • Tyrosinase: the TDS states that tranexamic acid has a structure similar to melanin and acts as a competitive inhibitor of tyrosinase, the key enzyme of melanin synthesis.
  • Kallikrein and dark circles: the TDS states that TXA inhibits kallikrein activity and the dilation of blood vessels, which is the reasoning behind its use in under-eye products for dark circles.

The practical result is a brightening active that works upstream (signals from keratinocytes), at the enzyme level (tyrosinase) and on the vascular component of discolouration. It is a good partner for actives that work elsewhere, such as niacinamide (melanosome transfer) or alpha arbutin (tyrosinase).

What is TranEX-1 (tranexamoyl dipeptide-23)?

TranEX-1 is a "vitamin-peptide": tranexamic acid is introduced onto the N-terminus of a dipeptide by synthesis. A&PEP's position is that the peptide part "plays a role in doubling the whitening effect", and the lab and clinical data below were generated to test that claim against TXA on its own. The company applied for the patent on 29 April 2016; it was registered on 9 April 2018, and the technology is also granted in the United States as US 10,532,018 ("Method of whitening skin using tranexamic acid-peptide having skin whitening activity"). TranEX-1 received a Best Patent Product award in Korea in 2018.

Lab data: melanin, MITF and cell safety

Melanin inhibition assay

In A&PEP's melanin inhibition assay, melanin content was measured with the positive (+) control set at 100%. TranEX-1 was tested from 125 to 2,000 ppm next to two well-known references: arbutin and free tranexamic acid. At every concentration TranEX-1 left less melanin than TXA, and at most concentrations less than arbutin. At 2,000 ppm, melanin fell to about 30% with TranEX-1, compared with about 34% for arbutin and about 41% for TXA.

The gap is largest at the lower doses. At 125 ppm TranEX-1 kept melanin near 71%, while arbutin and TXA were close to 90% and 93%, which suggests the peptide form is more effective weight for weight.

MITF gene expression (qRT-PCR)

MITF is the master transcription factor that switches on tyrosinase and other melanin genes. A&PEP measured MITF expression by quantitative real-time PCR, using GAPDH as the reference gene, at 0.1% and 0.05% TranEX-1. The TDS reports that MITF expression was suppressed to 62.19% at 20 cycles, i.e. the brightening effect is not only enzyme inhibition but also lower expression of the genes that build melanin.

Cell safety

Cytotoxicity was tested on human keratinocytes (HaCaT) and mouse fibroblasts (3T3) from 31.3 to 1,000 ppm. Cell viability stayed at roughly 90% or higher at every concentration, and the supplier concludes that TranEX-1 "showed no toxicity in the applied dosage". As with any new formula, the finished product still needs its own safety assessment and patch testing.

Clinical data: 8-week melanin index

A&PEP's clinical test enrolled adults in their 30s to 50s with freckles and dark circles. The test product was applied to the whole face and freckle zone at least five times a week (up to seven, depending on skin age and freckle density) for 4 to 8 weeks, and every subject used sunscreen during the study. Melanin index was measured and the change from week 0 calculated as [(M0 − Mw)/M0] × 100.

After 8 weeks, melanin index had fallen by 26.9% with TranEX-1, against 11.4% with TXA and 6.6% with the control cream. The supplier reports a whitening improvement rate of 235.9% compared with the control group. Two points matter for formulators reading this:

  • The TranEX-1 curve keeps rising through week 8 without levelling off, while the TXA curve flattens. Consumers should be told to use a tranexamic acid serum for at least 8 weeks.
  • Sunscreen was used throughout, and the control group also improved a little. Daily sun protection is part of any brightening routine, and your product claims should reflect that.

The TDS does not state the number of subjects or the exact test concentration, so treat these figures as supplier data that supports, rather than replaces, testing of your own finished product.

TranEX-1 also combines well with other brighteners. A kojic acid and tranexamic acid serum, an alpha arbutin and tranexamic acid combination, or tranexamic acid with vitamin C are all popular on Amazon India. Pairing actives that work at different steps (signals, enzyme, gene expression, melanin transfer) is a sound way to get visible results without pushing any one active to irritating levels.

How to formulate a tranexamic acid serum with TranEX-1

A simple, stable TranEX-1 serum follows the usual water-based route:

  1. Water phase: deionised water with a humectant (glycerin, butylene glycol or propanediol), a chelating agent and a light gel former such as a polymer or gum.
  2. Hydrate and neutralise the thickener, then cool below 40 °C.
  3. Add TranEX-1 at 3–7% with gentle stirring. Add other heat-sensitive actives (niacinamide, alpha arbutin, encapsulated vitamin C, soothing agents) at this stage.
  4. Preserve. TranEX-1 contains no added preservative, so the finished product needs a complete preservative system and a challenge test.
  5. Adjust pH to about 4.5–6.0, close to TranEX-1's own pH range of 4.0–6.0, and check for haze, since the raw material can be slightly hazy.

Formulation tips

  • Keep the heat low. Peptides are best added in the cool-down phase rather than during heating.
  • Avoid extremes of pH. Very acidic exfoliating serums are not the ideal home for a peptide; put AHA products and TranEX-1 products in separate steps of a routine if needed.
  • Creams and lotions: add TranEX-1 to the water phase after emulsification, below 40 °C.
  • Under-eye gels: the kallikrein mechanism in the TDS supports TranEX-1 for dark-circle products; pair it with caffeine or soothing actives.
  • Packaging: airless or pump packs reduce contamination risk in a water-rich serum.
  • Claims: in India, brighteners are cosmetics, so use cosmetic claims ("visibly evens skin tone", "reduces the look of dark spots") and keep medical words such as "treats melasma" out of your labels and listings.

Frequently asked questions

What does a tranexamic acid serum do for skin?

It helps even out skin tone and reduce the look of dark spots. Tranexamic acid slows the plasmin pathway that UV light switches on in keratinocytes, so fewer signals reach melanocytes to make extra melanin, and it also calms the vascular redness that often sits under pigmented patches.

What is tranexamoyl dipeptide-23?

It is the INCI name of TranEX-1 from A&PEP, Korea: tranexamic acid chemically bonded to the N-terminus of a dipeptide. The supplier's data shows lower melanin than free tranexamic acid at the same concentrations in the lab, and a 26.9% melanin index reduction after 8 weeks against 11.4% for tranexamic acid.

How much TranEX-1 should I use in a serum?

A&PEP's current datasheet recommends 3–7% of the supplied liquid, which contains 1,000 ppm (0.1%) peptide. Add it to the water phase below 40 °C and keep the finished product at about pH 4.5–6.0.

Can I combine tranexamic acid with niacinamide, alpha arbutin, kojic acid or vitamin C?

Yes. These actives work at different steps of pigmentation, so combinations are common. Check pH compatibility, add each active in the cool-down phase and run stability tests on the full formula, especially with vitamin C, which needs its own protection from oxidation.

How long does a tranexamic acid serum take to show results?

In the supplier's clinical test, melanin index fell steadily from week 2 and was still improving at week 8, with daily sunscreen use. Brands should advise consumers to use the product for at least 8 weeks.

Is TranEX-1 safe for sensitive skin?

In A&PEP's cell tests on human keratinocytes and fibroblasts, viability stayed around 90% or higher up to 1,000 ppm, and the supplier reports no toxicity at the doses tested. Every finished product still needs its own safety assessment, preservative challenge test and patch testing.

Does TranEX-1 contain a preservative?

No. The datasheet lists it as preservative-free, so the finished cosmetic must include a complete preservative system suited to a water-based serum or cream.
