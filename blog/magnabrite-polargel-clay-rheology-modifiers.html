Magnabrite®, Polargel® and Amazonian clays: choosing the right cosmetic clay for creams, gels and masks

Mineral clays were thickening cosmetics long before synthetic polymers arrived, and they are back in demand as brands look for natural, plastic-free rheology. Here is what the HBS technical data says about Magnabrite® (magnesium aluminium silicate) and Polargel® (bentonite), how Bielus's coloured Amazonian clays from Brazil fit alongside them, and how to pick the right grade.

What these clays actually are

Both families are smectite clays: minerals built from ultra-thin platelets of silica and alumina. HBS (AMCOL, part of Minerals Technologies) mines them at Belle Fourche, South Dakota, USA, where the deposits formed from weathered volcanic ash. The ore is then water-washed and purified to pharmaceutical standards.

  • Magnabrite® — INCI Magnesium Aluminum Silicate. White smectite minerals picked and blended for consistency, usually shipped as small flakes that wet out quickly.
  • Polargel® — INCI Bentonite. Selectively mined sodium bentonite (montmorillonite), supplied as a white micro-fine powder; Polargel® CA is a calcium bentonite for masks.

Drop the platelets into water with good shear and they separate. Each gram opens up more than 750 m² of surface. The platelet faces carry a negative charge and the edges a slight positive charge, so they link edge-to-face into a loose "house of cards" network. That network holds water, keeps pigments and particles from settling and stops emulsions from separating, yet it breaks down under shear, so the product spreads easily and then rebuilds at rest.

What they do in a formula

  • Thicken water phases without a sticky or stringy feel; the TDS describes the texture as soft and slippery.
  • Suspend insoluble particles such as zinc oxide, titanium dioxide, pigments, exfoliating beads and encapsulated actives.
  • Stabilise emulsions, so creams and lotions hold together over shelf life.
  • Absorb oil — the reason clays are used in mattifying and acne-care products and in face masks.
  • Work with gums. Clay and xanthan gum are a classic pair: the gum's slipperiness is reduced and the clay network gains body. HBS's own Microsponge® 520RA example cream uses magnesium aluminium silicate with xanthan gum.

Magnabrite® grades: chosen by viscosity and tolerance

All Magnabrite® grades share the same chemistry, but the ratio of aluminium to magnesium and the processing change how thick they get and what they tolerate. The chart shows each grade's viscosity specification at 5% solids, taken from the HBS technical data sheets and product specifications.

The second difference is mineral make-up. As the Al/Mg ratio climbs from Magnabrite® S to K to HS, the clay carries less magnesium and more alumina. In practice that shifts each grade toward a different job: S and K are flagged for acidic formulas (low acid demand, high acid compatibility), while HS was developed to hydrate in hot or cold water and stay stable when electrolytes are present.

Polargel® grades: bentonite for body, masks and sun care

Polargel® is processed bentonite. HBS lists it as a viscosifier, suspending agent, binder and emulsion stabiliser, with specialty grades that absorb skin oils in masks or boost the SPF of sunscreens.

Bielus Amazonian clays: colour and purification from Brazil

Magnabrite® and Polargel® are engineered for structure: they are purified to pharmaceutical grade and chosen mainly for viscosity and suspension. The coloured clays from Bielus Ingredientes in Brazil do a different job. They are kaolin-based clays (INCI Kaolin) whose colour comes from the minerals they naturally contain, such as iron oxides, manganese and different clay types. They are the "hero" of a clay mask, cleanser or scrub, the ingredient a consumer sees on the label and on the skin.

Combining Bielus clays with Magnabrite® or Polargel®

The two types work well together. A cream mask built only on coloured kaolin tends to be stiff, dry quickly on the skin and separate in the jar. Adding a small amount of Magnabrite® or Polargel® to the water phase gives it body and spreadability, keeps the coloured clay evenly suspended, and stops water separating out over shelf life. For a rinse-off mask, try a base of Polargel® CA or Polargel® NF with one or two Bielus clays for colour and purifying claims, and pair with Amazonian oils or butters for comfort.

Which clay? A quick decision guide

  • White, light-textured cream or lotion — start with Magnabrite® S, or HV if you want body at a low use level.
  • Acidic formula (AHA, BHA, vitamin C) — Magnabrite® S or K, which are designed for low acid demand; Polargel® NF is also listed for AHA/BHA creams.
  • Salt-heavy or high-electrolyte formula — Magnabrite® HS.
  • Face mask or oil-control product — Polargel® CA as the base, with Bielus Green or Black Clay for purifying claims and colour.
  • Sensitive-skin mask — Bielus White or Old Rose Clay, with Magnabrite® S for a smooth, stable texture.
  • Radiance or "glow" mask — Bielus Red or Golden Clay.
  • Naturally coloured product without dyes — any Bielus clay, alone or blended.
  • Mineral sunscreen — Magnabrite® S or Polargel® UV to keep zinc oxide and titanium dioxide suspended.
  • Roll-on deodorant — Magnabrite® HV or Polargel® HV.

Quality and regulatory documents

Because these grades are also sold into pharmaceuticals, the paperwork is unusually complete. From the statements HBS has issued:

  • Microbiology: specification of <10 CFU/g aerobic count and <10 CFU/g yeast and mould, with E. coli, S. aureus, P. aeruginosa, Salmonella and gram negatives absent in 10 g. Products are irradiated in their final packaging as the last manufacturing step.
  • Heavy metals: arsenic ≤3 ppm and lead ≤15 ppm, tested on every lot and shown on the CoA. A California Prop 65 guidance letter is available.
  • Free-from: parabens, formaldehyde, latex, EU-listed fragrance allergens, USP <467> residual solvents (including ethylene oxide) and metal catalysts.
  • Origin: naturally derived mineral, with no animal or plant-derived materials, which suits vegan and natural positioning.

On safety, the Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) Expert Panel assessed magnesium aluminium silicate, bentonite and related clays in 2003 and found them safe as used in cosmetics. In the animal tests it reviewed, magnesium aluminium silicate caused at most mild skin irritation, and a week of daily use on human skin showed no problems. A 2021 CIR update on silicates reached the same conclusion, provided the finished product is designed not to irritate.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Magnabrite and Polargel?

Magnabrite is magnesium aluminium silicate, a purified blend of white smectite clays. Polargel is bentonite, a purified sodium (or calcium) montmorillonite clay. Both thicken, suspend and stabilise water-based formulas; Magnabrite grades are chosen by viscosity and acid or electrolyte tolerance, Polargel grades by viscosity and by special uses such as clay masks and sunscreens.

Which Magnabrite grade gives the highest viscosity?

Magnabrite HV. Its specification is 800–2200 cps at 5% solids, compared with 225–600 cps for Magnabrite S and 40–200 cps for Magnabrite HS.

Are Magnabrite and Polargel natural?

Yes. HBS certifies both as mined minerals that formed from volcanic ash over geological time, so nothing in them comes from animals or plants. Its statements also confirm they contain no parabens, formaldehyde, latex or EU fragrance allergens.

What are Amazonian clays used for in cosmetics?

Bielus Amazonian clays from Brazil (INCI Kaolin) are used mainly in face and body masks, cleansers and scrubs, where they absorb oil and give a natural colour from their own mineral oxides. They are not designed as thickeners; for viscosity and suspension, pair them with Magnabrite or Polargel.
