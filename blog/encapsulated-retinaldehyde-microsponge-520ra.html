Encapsulated retinaldehyde: how Microsponge® 520RA delivers slower release and better stability

Retinaldehyde (retinal) sits one step closer to retinoic acid than retinol, but it is light- and oxygen-sensitive and can be irritating. Here is what the technical data shows when it is entrapped in a Microsponge® delivery system, and how to formulate with it.

Why retinaldehyde instead of retinol?

Skin converts every retinoid it receives into retinoic acid, the form that actually signals to skin cells. Retinol needs two oxidation steps to get there: first to retinaldehyde, then to retinoic acid. Retinaldehyde needs only the second step, so it starts one step closer to the active form.

For brands, that makes retinal attractive for anti-ageing serums and night creams: it behaves as a cosmetic ingredient rather than a drug, while working further along the conversion pathway than retinol.

The two problems with free retinal

  • Instability. Retinoids oxidise and isomerise in light and air, so potency can drop both in the raw-material drum and in the finished pack.
  • Irritation. A fast burst of retinoid on the skin is a common cause of redness and peeling, which makes consumers stop using a product before it has time to work.

Microsponge® 520RA addresses both by entrapping retinaldehyde inside porous acrylate microspheres. The yellow, free-flowing powder carries 20% ± 2% retinaldehyde and disperses in gels, emulsions and powders.

Release: slower, steadier delivery into the skin model

In a Franz diffusion-cell study reported in the manufacturer's TDS, two creams each containing 0.10% retinaldehyde were compared: one with free retinal added to the oil phase and one with the same amount of retinal supplied in the Microsponge® system. The entrapped retinal crossed the skin-mimicking membrane more slowly. By the final time point it had delivered about 24 µg/cm² compared with about 37 µg/cm² for free retinal, roughly a third less.

Sustained release is the point: the same total dose reaches the skin over a longer period, and the TDS notes that similar testing with other irritating actives has shown sustained release can reduce irritation and improve how consistently consumers use anti-ageing products.

Stability as a raw material

The powder was stored at ambient conditions under inert gas and sampled repeatedly over 44 weeks, with the container opened at each time point. Retinaldehyde content, measured by HPLC, stayed within the 18–22% specification throughout the study.

Stability in a finished cream

A cream with 0.10% retinaldehyde from 520RA was packed in 30 mL foil-laminate tubes and held at 40 °C for 12 weeks. Retinal content stayed at about 0.10–0.13% across the test, which the manufacturer states supports a two-year shelf life.

Clinical results: what encapsulated retinaldehyde did in 8 weeks

HBS, the manufacturer of Microsponge® 520RA, ran an 8-week clinical study on its sister grade Microsponge® N 720TRA. That grade carries the same 20% retinaldehyde, but in a natural cellulose sponge instead of 520RA's polymer sponge. Twenty women aged 36–55 applied a lotion with 0.15% retinal (from the Microsponge® N 720TRA grade) to the whole face. The study was run by Essex Laboratories.

  • A statistically significant reduction in fine lines and wrinkles appeared after just 2 weeks (p < 0.001 vs baseline), and the reduction kept growing until week 8.
  • No visible skin irritation was recorded.
  • 94% of panelists felt their skin was smoother, and 81% saw an improved appearance of wrinkles.

These results are for the 720TRA grade. Microsponge® 520RA uses the same encapsulation principle and the same retinaldehyde loading, so the study shows what encapsulated retinal can do. It is not a test of 520RA itself. Ask Kalptaru Naturals for the full HBS study and the grade that suits your formula.

How to formulate with Microsponge® 520RA

StepGuidance
DoseThe TDS example cream delivers 0.10% retinaldehyde. At 20% loading that is about 0.5% Microsponge® 520RA.
When to addAdd during the cool-down phase of an emulsion, not to the hot phase.
LightHandle under yellow light to prevent isomerisation.
OxygenSparge the batch with nitrogen and keep air exposure to a minimum.
Opened packsUse the full pack once opened, or reseal it straight away.
PackagingUse airless or laminate tubes. The stability test used foil-laminate tubes.

The manufacturer's example cream combines caprylic/capric triglyceride, glycerin, cetearyl alcohol, polysorbate 60, magnesium aluminium silicate and xanthan gum, with tocopheryl acetate, ascorbyl palmitate and BHT as antioxidants, and bisabolol for comfort. Frequently asked questions

Is retinaldehyde stronger than retinol?

Retinaldehyde needs one conversion step to become retinoic acid, while retinol needs two, so it starts closer to the active form. How a product performs still depends on dose, formulation and stability.

How much Microsponge 520RA should I use?

The manufacturer's example cream contains 0.10% retinaldehyde, which is about 0.5% Microsponge® 520RA at its 20% loading. Ask us for the TDS to set the level for your product.

Can Microsponge 520RA go into the hot phase?

No. Add it during cool-down, handle it under yellow light and keep air exposure low, for example by sparging with nitrogen.
